# "The Detail" — domain, company and trademark check

Date of search: 31 July 2026. Preliminary screening only — see [Limitations](#limitations).

## Summary

The name is **heavily occupied**. Every strong domain is gone, at least five
active companies trade under a form of "The Detail", and the phrase is weak as
a trademark because it is ordinary descriptive English. This is not a fatal
finding, but it means the name will be expensive to own and hard to defend.

## 3. Trademark position

No exact registered word mark for **"THE DETAIL"** surfaced in any register
searched. That sounds encouraging; it mostly is not, for two reasons.

**a. The phrase is inherently weak.** "The Detail" is ordinary descriptive
English. Under s.3(1) of the UK Trade Marks Act 1994 (and the EUIPO/USPTO
equivalents), marks that are descriptive or non-distinctive for the relevant
goods or services are refused. The more your business is *about* detail —
design, finishing, cleaning, consultancy, quality assurance — the more likely a
plain word-mark application is refused outright. In the strongest sectors you'd
be pushed toward a **figurative mark** (the word plus a specific logo treatment),
which protects the styling far more than the name.

**b. Weak marks are hard to enforce.** Even if registered, you would have narrow
scope. You could not stop the five-plus incumbents above, and they could not
easily stop each other. Everyone coexists in a crowded field.

## 4. What to do

**If you keep the name**

1. Decide the sector first — everything below depends on it.
2. Register a figurative mark (wordmark + logo), not the bare words. Given the
   existing "VC." house style, a distinctive full-stop lockup — **The Detail.** —
   is a real asset here: the styling is registrable even where the words aren't.
3. Budget for a paid clearance search in your actual classes before filing.
   UK IPO fees are £170–200 for one class, +£50 per extra class; a solicitor-run
   clearance is separate and worth it in a field this crowded.
4. Add a distinguishing element. "The Detail Co.", "The Detail Studio" and
   "The Detail Group" are all taken; something sector-specific and unused is
   both more registrable and more findable.
5. Accept a compromise domain. `thedetail.app` or `thedetail.works` are clean,
   but you will fight for search results against five incumbents forever.

**If you're open to changing it**

A coined or unexpected word would cost far less over the life of the business:
a `.com` you can actually buy, a mark that registers on the first attempt, and
a search result you own. Given how crowded "The Detail" is, this is the option
I'd lean toward.

## Limitations

- Domain availability was checked through a registrar API. "Unavailable" almost
  always means registered, but for some ccTLDs (`.ae`, `.co.uk`, `.uk`) it can
  also mean that registrar does not sell the TLD. Confirm those three with a
  WHOIS lookup or the local registry (`.ae` via aeDA, `.uk` via Nominet) before
  concluding they're taken.
- The official register search interfaces (USPTO TSDR/TESS, UK IPO, EUIPO
  eSearch, Companies House) blocked automated access during this check. Company
  and trademark data here comes from search results and third-party trademark
  aggregators, and should be re-verified directly on the official registers.
- The absence of an exact "THE DETAIL" mark is a *negative from a partial
  search* — it is not proof that none exists, particularly outside the US/UK/EU.
- **This is a screening exercise, not legal advice.** A registered trademark
  attorney should run formal clearance in your chosen classes and jurisdictions
  before you commit spend to the name.
